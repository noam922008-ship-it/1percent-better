How to stop scrolling on your phone for no reason

You open your phone to check one thing, and suddenly half an hour is gone. That isn't a lack of discipline. Apps are built so it's hard to stop: the feed never ends and there's always one more video.

The good news: you don't need crazy willpower. You need a few small changes that make scrolling a little less automatic.

1. Notice when it happens

Most scrolling happens at the same times: right after waking up, in bed before sleep, when you're bored or putting something off. For one day, just notice when it happens to you. No need to change anything yet.

2. Decide in advance what you do instead

This is the most important step. Instead of "I won't scroll", decide: "When I feel like scrolling for no reason, I ___". For example:

  • read 2 pages of a book
  • go for a 5-minute walk
  • put the phone in another room for half an hour

The replacement should be too small to fail. "If X happens, I do Y" plans have a lot of research behind them showing they help people stick to decisions.

3. Move the apps off your home screen

An app you have to look for is harder to open without thinking. Move the apps you scroll most into a folder on the last page, or take them off the home screen completely.

4. Turn off notifications

Every notification is an invitation to pick up the phone. Keep notifications only from real people and turn off the rest: likes, "you might like", updates.

5. Set a time limit

Your phone has a built-in tool for app time limits. On iPhone it's in Settings → Screen Time → App Limits, and on Android it's usually under "Digital Wellbeing". Set a limit for your scrolling apps, for example 30 minutes a day. When time runs out the phone asks if you want to continue, and that moment of pause already helps.

6. The phone stays out of bed

If most of your scrolling happens before sleep, charge the phone outside the bedroom or far from the bed. If it's also your alarm clock, a simple alarm clock is cheap.

7. Count days, not perfection

There will be scrolling days. That's fine. What matters is coming back the next day. Count the days without mindless scrolling: a number that goes up every day gives you a reason to keep going.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to stop scrolling?

It's different for everyone. For most people the first couple of weeks are the hardest, and after that it becomes less automatic.

Do I have to delete the apps?

No. Usually moving them off the home screen and setting a time limit is enough. If one app really pulls you in, delete it for a week and see how it feels.

What do I do when I'm bored?

That's exactly what step 2 is for. When the decision is made in advance, you don't have to think in the moment.
