How to stop eating junk food out of habit

Most junk food isn't eaten because you're hungry. It's eaten because you're bored, tired, because it's sitting on the table, or because that's what you do in front of a show.

That isn't a lack of discipline. It's a habit, and a habit can be changed in small steps.

1. Notice when it happens

In front of a screen, after school or work, late at night, when you're stressed. For one day, just notice when it happens, without changing anything.

2. Decide in advance what you do instead

"When I feel like junk for no reason, I ___". For example:

  • drink a glass of water first
  • eat a piece of fruit
  • go for a 5-minute walk

And if you still want it after that, that's allowed. The goal is for it not to happen on autopilot.

3. If it's not at home, it doesn't get eaten

It's hard to resist a snack sitting in the drawer. Simply don't buy it for home, or keep it somewhere hard to reach.

4. Don't get too hungry

When you skip a meal, hunger wins in the evening. Regular meals during the day reduce cravings at night.

5. Water first

Thirst often feels like hunger. A glass of water before opening the fridge gives you a moment to think.

6. Don't eat from the bag in front of a screen

When you eat straight from the bag in front of a show, you don't notice how much. Put it in a small bowl, and that's it.

7. Count days, not perfection

There will be junk days, like a birthday or going out with friends, and that's fine. No need to punish yourself, just come back the next day.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to quit completely?

No. The goal is for it not to happen automatically every day, not to give it up forever.

What do I do when the craving is really strong?

Wait 10 minutes and do something else in the meantime. Often the craving just passes.

What if all my friends are eating?

It's fine to join in. The habit is built in what happens day to day, not on one night out.
